How the routing math changed on 12 Aug 2026 — and why it traps some responders

Same three inputs decide who gets asked first, both before and after 4.2. What changed is how much each one counts, and how long an offer waits for an answer — and that combination created a one-way trap that didn't exist before.

Before 4.2 — through 11 Aug

Ranking weights and offer window

90s offer window
Proximity 45%
Recent acceptance 40%
Capability match 15%

Distance and reliability counted about the same. A responder a bit farther away could still rank near the top on a strong recent-acceptance record. A 90-second window meant most people had time to notice a buzz before it moved on.

After 4.2 — from 12 Aug

Ranking weights and offer window

60s offer window ↓ from 90s
Proximity 60%
Recent acceptance 25%
Capability match 15%

Distance now dominates. Being farther away is much harder to make up for with a good record. And with only 60 seconds to answer, more offers get missed by accident — each miss dings the same score that can no longer buy back much rank.

The trap this combination creates

This scoring rule hasn't changed since 2019: a decline or a timeout lowers a responder's score, and nothing brings it back up except accepting a job. That rule only became a trap once distance started dominating the ranking and the answer window got shorter — together they cut off the way back.

1You rank lower, so the offer goes to someone else
2Fewer offers reach you at all
3The rare offer has only 60s — easier to miss
4A miss lowers your score again — it never rises on its own

Only way out: a nearby incident where you rank high enough anyway, despite the lower score.

What this produced: 4 of 16 responders lost 80%+ of their weekly offers after 4.2 while the total number of offers sent stayed about the same — the work didn't disappear, it moved to whoever was already winning. See 4.2-offer-redistribution.html for the per-responder numbers.

Source: 00-rook/code/dispatch-routing/config.py and history.py (weights, timeout, scoring rule) and 00-rook/data/callout-history.csv (redistribution figures). "Recent acceptance never decays on its own" is an open TODO in history.py dated 2019, unresolved as of 4.2.
