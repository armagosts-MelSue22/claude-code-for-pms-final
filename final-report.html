What actually happened

Plainly: it's a trap, not a slowdown

Release 4.2 (12 Aug) cut the callout timeout from 90s to 60s and reweighted routing to favor proximity over recent-acceptance history — both deliberate. Neither one, on its own, explains what happened next.

↩ loops back to the top — with no offers, there is nothing to accept

Farlight · The Undertow · Vesper · Meteor Mite
Lost 79–89% of their weekly offers after 4.2 and never recovered — while ten other responders on the same roster gained 19–49%. Total offers sent stayed flat. This is a redistribution problem, not a volume problem.
history.py, 2019 → today
A comment beside record_declined() asked whether the score should ease back toward neutral on its own. It was left unresolved: "leaving it as-is for now." 4.2 is what made that gap matter.

What the solution is

A way back, not a rebuild

This deliberately doesn't touch the 4.2 reweighting or the 60-second timeout — those are separate, already-understood decisions. It only closes the gap Wen flagged in 2019: nothing brings a parked score back on its own.

  • history.pyCore of the change — add a per-responder last-updated timestamp (doesn't exist today) and decay logic, computed lazily inside recent_acceptance().
  • config.pyTwo new tunables alongside ACCEPTANCE_CREDIT / DECLINE_PENALTY: how long unoffered before decay starts, how big each step is.
  • offer.py / routing.pyNo changes expected — routing already reads the decayed value automatically once history.py produces it.
  • availability.pyNo changes expected, but any shape change to a responder record goes through Supply first, per that file's own note.
How we'll know it worked

Farlight, The Undertow, Vesper, and Meteor Mite each return to within their pre-4.2 weekly offer-volume band — not just off the floor — within roughly 6–8 weeks of ship, with visible movement expected by week 4. Roster-wide acceptance rate recovering on its own isn't sufficient proof; it's already drifting up for reasons that look seasonal.

Won't happen again

4.2 shipped clean — no pages, no alarms — and still cost four people weeks of silence

The real lesson isn't just the decay bug. It's that a brief can look complete and still ship without an owner, a way to measure success, or an honest list of open questions — and nobody catches it until support tickets pile up. So the last thing built this course wasn't a product fix. It was a habit, turned into a tool.
